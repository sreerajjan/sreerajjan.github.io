Four-Lens Triangulation

A hypothesis about user behaviour is a claim. Claims need corroboration. Four-Lens Triangulation is the discipline of never accepting a product decision on one signal alone — and knowing which four signals, together, are usually enough.

The four lenses: behaviour (what the clickstream shows), voice (what users say in research or complaints), journey (what happens in the wider funnel around this moment), and outcome (what the business metric does after the change). Any single lens can be misleading; two agreeing lenses is coincidence; three or four aligning is a signal you can defend to a room full of skeptics.

The framework came out of hypothesis-testing work at Bajaj Finance where too many product bets were being made on partial evidence — a compelling survey stat here, a clickstream anomaly there — without the other two lenses ever getting a vote. It shows up in the Meridian case study and in most of the behavioural CX work I do.
